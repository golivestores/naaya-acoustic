Hangzhou, China

1,200 seats
Scope
Interior · Acoustic · AV · Build
Systems
Vega · Uniseam

The brief

A concert-grade room that still reads as architecture.

The client asked for a 1,200-seat auditorium that could move between unamplified symphonic work and fully amplified conferences — two acoustic targets that normally fight each other. NAAYA was appointed as the single point of responsibility across interior design, room acoustics, AV systems and construction.

Working from the architect’s concept, our studio developed the visual language while the acoustic team modelled the room toward a mid-band reverberation of 1.6 seconds occupied, with motorised variable absorption bringing speech mode down to 1.1 seconds.

Our approach

One contract, four disciplines, no hand-offs.

01

Interior design

The room’s visual language — grooved timber, brass detailing and a single warm palette — was developed in-house, so every acoustic decision had a place to live.

02

Acoustic engineering

Ray-tracing and finite-element models set absorption and diffusion per band; every wall build-up was prototyped and lab-measured before release.

03

AV & systems

Line arrays, stage monitoring and conference AV were designed against the same room model — not retrofitted after the architecture was fixed.

04

Construction

Our own installation crew built the room, with tolerances checked against the acoustic model at every stage and a measured commissioning at handover.

The space

Systems used

Our own systems, from factory to wall.

Wall system

Vega — grooved timber absorber

Slot-perforated panels tuned per band carried the hall’s mid-frequency absorption, in a custom walnut finish.

View the Vega series →
Ceiling system

Uniseam — seamless acoustic ceiling

A monolithic absorptive ceiling kept the foyer and circulation calm without reading as an acoustic product at all.

The result

Measured, not promised.

Commissioning measurements were taken across 24 seat positions in both room modes, occupied-condition figures verified against the model.
